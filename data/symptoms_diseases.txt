COMMON SYMPTOMS AND DISEASES

=== RESPIRATORY CONDITIONS ===

Common Cold:
Symptoms: Runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, mild cough, low-grade fever, mild fatigue.
Duration: 7-10 days.
Management: Rest, increased fluid intake, over-the-counter decongestants or antihistamines. Avoid antibiotics as the common cold is viral.
Prevention: Regular handwashing, avoiding close contact with infected individuals, not touching face with unwashed hands.

Influenza (Flu):
Symptoms: Sudden high fever (38-40°C), severe body aches, headache, fatigue, dry cough, chills, loss of appetite.
Duration: 1-2 weeks.
Management: Rest, fluids, antiviral medications (e.g., oseltamivir) if taken early, fever reducers like paracetamol or ibuprofen.
Prevention: Annual flu vaccination is the most effective prevention. Good hand hygiene and respiratory etiquette.

COVID-19:
Symptoms: Fever, dry cough, fatigue, loss of taste or smell, shortness of breath, body aches, sore throat. Symptoms range from mild to severe.
Management: Isolation, rest, hydration. Seek emergency care for difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, or confusion.
Prevention: Vaccination, masking in crowded places, hand hygiene, ventilation.

Pneumonia:
Symptoms: High fever with chills, productive cough (green/yellow or bloody mucus), chest pain when breathing, shortness of breath, rapid breathing, fatigue.
Risk factors: Age over 65, chronic disease, weakened immune system, smoking.
Management: Requires medical evaluation. Bacterial pneumonia treated with antibiotics. Rest and fluids essential.

Asthma:
Symptoms: Wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, persistent cough (especially at night or early morning).
Triggers: Allergens, exercise, cold air, smoke, respiratory infections.
Management: Inhaled bronchodilators (rescue inhalers), inhaled corticosteroids for long-term control. Avoid triggers.

=== CARDIOVASCULAR CONDITIONS ===

Hypertension (High Blood Pressure):
Normal BP: Below 120/80 mmHg.
High BP: 130/80 mmHg or higher.
Symptoms: Often called the "silent killer" - usually no symptoms. Severe cases: headaches, dizziness, nosebleeds.
Risk factors: Obesity, high salt diet, physical inactivity, smoking, alcohol, stress, family history.
Management: Lifestyle changes (low-sodium diet, exercise, weight loss), antihypertensive medications.

Heart Disease:
Symptoms of heart attack: Chest pain or pressure, pain radiating to arm/jaw/back, shortness of breath, nausea, sweating, dizziness.
IMPORTANT: Chest pain or pressure is a medical emergency - call emergency services immediately.
Prevention: Healthy diet, regular exercise, no smoking, managing blood pressure and cholesterol.

=== DIABETES ===

Type 2 Diabetes:
Symptoms: Frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, frequent infections, fatigue, tingling in hands/feet.
Risk factors: Overweight/obesity, physical inactivity, family history, age over 45, prediabetes.
Management: Healthy diet (low sugar, complex carbs), regular physical activity, blood sugar monitoring, medications (metformin, insulin if needed).
Prevention: Maintaining healthy weight, regular exercise, balanced diet, regular screening.

=== MENTAL HEALTH ===

Anxiety:
Symptoms: Excessive worry, restlessness, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbances.
Management: Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), stress management techniques, mindfulness, regular exercise. Medication may be recommended by a doctor.

Depression:
Symptoms: Persistent sadness, loss of interest in activities, changes in appetite or weight, sleep disturbances, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness, thoughts of death.
IMPORTANT: If experiencing thoughts of self-harm, seek immediate professional help or call a crisis helpline.
Management: Therapy (CBT, interpersonal therapy), antidepressant medications, lifestyle changes, social support.

=== DIGESTIVE CONDITIONS ===

Gastroenteritis (Stomach Flu):
Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomach cramps, low-grade fever.
Causes: Viral (norovirus, rotavirus) or bacterial infection.
Management: Oral rehydration therapy (ORT) - water, electrolytes. Avoid solid foods initially. BRAT diet (bananas, rice, applesauce, toast) when tolerated.
Warning signs: Signs of dehydration (dark urine, dizziness, dry mouth), blood in stool, high fever - seek medical care.

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS):
Symptoms: Abdominal pain, bloating, alternating diarrhea and constipation, mucus in stool.
Triggers: Stress, certain foods (dairy, gluten, fatty foods, caffeine).
Management: Dietary changes (low-FODMAP diet), stress management, fiber supplementation, probiotics.

=== MUSCULOSKELETAL CONDITIONS ===

Back Pain:
Common causes: Muscle strain, poor posture, herniated disc, arthritis.
Management: Rest (short-term), ice/heat therapy, over-the-counter pain relievers, gentle stretching, physical therapy.
Prevention: Regular exercise to strengthen core muscles, proper posture, ergonomic workspace.

Arthritis:
Types: Osteoarthritis (wear and tear), Rheumatoid arthritis (autoimmune).
Symptoms: Joint pain, stiffness, swelling, reduced range of motion.
Management: Exercise, weight management, pain relievers, anti-inflammatory medications. Medical supervision required for RA.

=== SKIN CONDITIONS ===

Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis):
Symptoms: Dry, itchy, inflamed skin; red or brownish-gray patches; small, raised bumps that may weep fluid.
Management: Moisturize regularly, avoid triggers (soaps, detergents, certain fabrics), topical corticosteroids for flares.

Acne:
Causes: Excess oil production, clogged pores, bacteria, inflammation.
Management: Gentle cleansing, non-comedogenic products, topical treatments (benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid). Severe cases need dermatologist consultation.
