FIRST AID GUIDANCE

IMPORTANT: First aid is intended to provide immediate assistance until professional medical help arrives. For any serious, life-threatening emergency, call emergency services (911 or your local emergency number) immediately.

=== CHOKING ===

Adult/Child (Over 1 year):
1. Ask "Are you choking?" - If they can cough, speak, or breathe, encourage them to keep coughing.
2. If they cannot cough, speak, or breathe: Call emergency services immediately.
3. Give 5 back blows: Stand behind the person, lean them forward, give 5 firm blows between shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
4. Give 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver): Stand behind the person, place one foot forward for stability. Make a fist, place it just above the navel (below the breastbone). Grab your fist with the other hand. Give 5 quick, upward thrusts.
5. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object is dislodged or the person loses consciousness.
6. If the person becomes unconscious, begin CPR.

Infant (Under 1 year):
1. Support infant face-down on your forearm.
2. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades.
3. Turn infant face-up on your other forearm.
4. Give 5 chest thrusts using 2 fingers on the center of the chest.
5. Alternate until object is dislodged or infant becomes unresponsive.

=== CPR (CARDIOPULMONARY RESUSCITATION) ===

When to perform: Person is unresponsive and not breathing normally (no breathing or only gasping).

Adult CPR (Hands-Only for untrained):
1. Call emergency services or have someone call while you begin CPR.
2. Place heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of breastbone).
3. Place other hand on top, interlace fingers.
4. Keep arms straight, compress chest at least 2 inches deep.
5. Push hard and fast: 100-120 compressions per minute (tempo of "Stayin' Alive").
6. Allow full chest recoil between compressions.
7. Continue until professional help arrives.

Full CPR (Trained - 30:2 ratio):
- 30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths.
- Open airway: head-tilt, chin-lift. Pinch nose, create seal, give breath over 1 second.

=== BURNS ===

Minor Burns (First-Degree - redness, pain, no blisters):
1. Cool the burn: Run cool (not cold/icy) water over the burn for 10-20 minutes.
2. Do not use ice, butter, toothpaste, or other home remedies.
3. Cover loosely with sterile gauze or clean cloth.
4. Take over-the-counter pain reliever if needed.
5. Do not pop any blisters.

Seek medical attention if:
- Burn is larger than 3 inches (size of palm).
- Burn is on face, hands, feet, genitals, buttocks, or over a major joint.
- Burn is deep (white or charred skin - third-degree).
- Caused by chemicals or electricity.
- Person is under 5 or over 60 years old.

=== BLEEDING ===

Controlling External Bleeding:
1. Apply direct pressure using a clean cloth, gauze, or bandage.
2. Maintain firm, continuous pressure for at least 15 minutes. Do not lift to check.
3. If blood soaks through, add more material on top (do not remove first layer).
4. Elevate the injured area above the heart if possible.
5. For severe, uncontrolled limb bleeding: Apply tourniquet 2-3 inches above the wound if trained to do so.

Seek emergency care for:
- Spurting blood (arterial bleeding).
- Bleeding that does not stop after 15 minutes of pressure.
- Deep or gaping wound.
- Signs of shock (pale, clammy skin, rapid breathing, dizziness, confusion).

=== FRACTURES AND SPRAINS ===

Suspected Fracture:
1. Immobilize the injured area - do not try to straighten.
2. Apply ice wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling (20 minutes on, 20 off).
3. Elevate if possible.
4. Seek medical attention.
Signs: Pain, swelling, bruising, deformity, inability to use the limb.

Sprain (RICE method):
- Rest: Avoid using the injured area.
- Ice: Apply ice pack wrapped in cloth for 20 minutes several times a day.
- Compression: Wrap with elastic bandage (not too tight).
- Elevation: Raise the injured area above heart level.
Seek care if severe pain, inability to bear weight, or significant swelling.

=== ALLERGIC REACTIONS ===

Mild Allergic Reaction:
Symptoms: Hives, itching, mild swelling, runny nose.
Management: Oral antihistamine (diphenhydramine/Benadryl). Monitor for worsening.

Anaphylaxis (SEVERE - Life-threatening Emergency):
Symptoms: Throat tightening, difficulty breathing, wheezing, rapid heartbeat, dizziness, severe hives, vomiting, loss of consciousness.
1. Call emergency services IMMEDIATELY (911).
2. Use epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) if available - inject into outer thigh.
3. Have the person lie down with legs elevated (unless breathing is difficult - sit up slightly).
4. A second dose of epinephrine can be given after 5-15 minutes if symptoms persist.
5. Even if symptoms improve, person must go to the hospital.

=== HEAT-RELATED ILLNESS ===

Heat Cramps (mildest):
Symptoms: Muscle cramps, usually in legs or abdomen.
Treatment: Stop activity, move to cool place, drink water or sports drink, gentle stretching.

Heat Exhaustion:
Symptoms: Heavy sweating, cold/pale/clammy skin, fast/weak pulse, nausea, weakness, dizziness, headache.
Treatment: Move to cool place, loosen clothing, apply cool wet cloths, sip cool water. Seek medical care if vomiting occurs.

Heat Stroke (EMERGENCY):
Symptoms: High body temperature (103°F+), hot/red/dry or damp skin, rapid/strong pulse, confusion, loss of consciousness.
1. Call emergency services immediately.
2. Move person to cool environment.
3. Cool person rapidly: ice packs to neck, armpits, groin; cool water immersion if possible.

=== POISONING ===

If you suspect poisoning:
1. Call Poison Control Center immediately (1-800-222-1222 in the US) or emergency services.
2. Do NOT induce vomiting unless instructed by poison control.
3. Note what was ingested, how much, and when.
4. If unconscious, not breathing, or having seizures: Call 911 immediately.

=== FAINTING ===

1. Help the person lie down or sit with head between knees.
2. Loosen tight clothing.
3. Do not give anything by mouth until fully conscious.
4. Elevate legs about 12 inches if possible.
5. Seek medical attention if the person does not recover quickly, is pregnant, has diabetes, or is over 50.

DISCLAIMER: This first aid information is for general educational purposes only. Always call emergency services for serious injuries or life-threatening situations. Take a certified first aid and CPR course for proper training.
